We downloaded the Bankwise ATM/POS/Card Statistics monthly data for 2025, cleaned it and combined it into a single dataset called combined_atm_data.csv in the previous post. We will use this dataset for our some descriptive analysis in this post.

In summary, the quick findings from the data are as follows:

  1. Private and Public sector banks have a much larger number of on-site ATMs compared to foreign banks.
  2. Private banks lead in the number of credit cards while public banks lead in the number of debit cards.
  3. Some small banks like Equitas Finance has a much higher and comparable average payment size to private and public sector banks. Even among private sector banks, some of the smaller banks have much higher average payment sizes (Karur Vysya Bank and J&K Bank).
  4. In terms of growth, the list is slightly different. Amex, Tamilnad Mercantile, Indian overseas Bank have high growth rates in card payment size. However, most banks saw a decline in average credit card payment size in 2025.
  5. Compared to private and public sector banks, foreign bank have very high average debit card payment size.
  6. Further, foreign banks have a much higher average debit card payment size compared to their credit card payment counterpart. Multiple factors likely explain this trend - perhaps high fees, charges or interest rate that discourage credit use, or perhaps the banks themselves are risk averse in the unsecured credit card lending space.

Average size of card payments

A slightly more interesting variable to look at is the average size of card payments, which can be calculated as the total value of card payments divided by the number of credit cards. This can give us insights into the spending behavior of cardholders and how it varies across different banks and over time.

While this also hints at the quality of the card portfolio of a bank, as higher average payment size could indicate more affluent customers or more active card usage, it also reflects the risk taking appetite of the bank in terms of its credit card business.

Assuming c_card_pymts_pos_val_000 is the total value of card payments and credit_cards_no is the number of credit cards, we can calculate the average size of card payments.

The below plot shows the average size of card payments over time by bank type (private, public, foreign). This can help us understand if there are any differences in the spending behavior of cardholders across different types of banks and how it has evolved over time.

# Calculate growth rate in avg_card_payment_size for each bank
import numpy as np

# Ensure data is sorted by time_period
dat0 = dat0.sort_values(['bank_name', 'time_period'])

# Calculate growth rate: (last - first) / first
growth_df = (
    dat0.groupby('bank_name')
    .agg(
        first_period=('time_period', 'first'),
        last_period=('time_period', 'last'),
        first_value=('avg_card_payment_size', 'first'),
        last_value=('avg_card_payment_size', 'last'),
        bank_type=('bank_type', 'first')
    )
    .reset_index()
)

growth_df['growth_rate'] = (growth_df['last_value'] - growth_df['first_value']) / growth_df['first_value']

# Remove banks with missing or zero first_value to avoid division by zero
growth_df = growth_df.replace([np.inf, -np.inf], np.nan).dropna(subset=['growth_rate'])

This can give us insights into the spending behavior of debit cardholders and how it varies across different banks and over time. It can also reflect the risk taking appetite of the bank in terms of its debit card business.

Compared to private and public sector banks, foreign bank have very high average debit card payment size, which could be a reflection of the fact that debit cards are often linked to salary accounts and used for smaller transactions. The average size of debit card payments for private and public sector banks has been relatively stable over time, while foreign banks have seen more fluctuations, which could be due to their different customer base and transaction patterns. It is interesting to note that foreign banks have a much higher average debit card payment size compared to credit card payment size. Multiple factors possible to explain this trend - perhaps high fees or detrimental charges or interest rate on deferring payment, or maybe the banks themselves are more risk averse in the unsecured credit card lending.

In conclusion, the analysis of the RBI’s Bankwise ATM/POS/Card Statistics data reveals significant insights into the distribution and growth of ATM infrastructure, as well as the dynamics of credit and debit card usage across different types of banks in India. The findings highlight the dominance of private and public sector banks in terms of ATM presence, while also shedding light on the varying trends in card payment sizes and growth rates among different banks. This analysis can serve as a valuable resource for understanding the evolving landscape of banking services and consumer behavior in India.
