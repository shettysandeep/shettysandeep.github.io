India 2026: Bonds over Equities?

Some thoughts on why 2026 may not be a good year for Indian equities and why debt instruments may be a better choice.
opinion
finance
Author

sandeep

Published

March 26, 2026

Geopolitical events with no clear end in sight, tectonic shifts in technology, and the vie for its hegemony by the power-hungry is rewriting investment decisions around the world. This churn will increase volatility further in the equity markets. Looking for capital preservation with predictable returns in 2026 is completely reasonable and recommended.

Waning interest of FIIs

Since 2024, FIIs have been selling indian equities. In 2025 they net sold Rs 1.2 lac crore, and in the first three months of 2026, they offloaded Rs 1 lac crore (exceeding 2025’s trends). FIIs ignored the GST reduction, the strong corporate earnings, the improving economic indicators, as well the various growth-inducive measures announced in the Union Budget FY2027 and the recent India-EU free trade agreement (FTA). They have sold on the backdrop of a falling rupee indicating no revival in the rupee in the short run or medium term - implying the dollar will remain strong for the foreseeable future making indian equities less attractive for foreign investors.

Liquidity tightening

To stall the falling rupee, RBI intervened on several ocassions - sold dollars and bought rupees. These interventions come at a cost. It sucks liquidity out of the market. While RBI infused liquidity back into the system via OMO of about Rs 4.2 trillion, and maintained soft stance on interest rates, the liquidity conditions is getting tight.

Yields have experienced an upward trend since November 2025, with the benchmark 10-year yield rising from around 6.54% in November 2025 to over 6.8% by late March 2026, hitting 16-month highs. High Credit-deposit ratios in the banking sector and cash demand will continue to put pressure on liquidity in the system, which will continue to push bond yields higher. RBI recently cancelled two T-Bill issuance on account of elevated bids. Front-loading government borrowing in the first quarter of the coming FY will further push yields higher.

West Asia conflict, energy prices

The Israel-US-Iran conflict has destabilized the middle east, shocked the energy supply chain and pushed energy prices up. It will remain so for the near to medium term, and we are yet to see or assess the impact of the instability in the middle east, the strained relationships due to Iran’s retaliation. The energy dynamics in this region will heighten uncertainty around india’s energy supplies and prices. Higher energy prices will put pressure on inflation whether or not it is passed through fully. The effects will surface in the next few months.

Inflation expectations, interest rate pressure

High inflation due to energy prices will likely push RBI towards a more hawkish stance. To rein inflation expectations, we might see rate hikes in the coming months. Higher interest rates will dampen sentiments, demand, and earnings.

Even if the cascade of events become less negative, it is unlikely the Indian equity markets will show a promising year. Weakening rupee, geopolitical frictions, FIIs on the sidelines, and exaggerated valuation of indian stocks will generate likely keep equity markets muted.

Attractiveness of Bonds, potential tax reliefs

Higher bond yields will be an attractive option for capital preservation in the medium term (2-3 years.) At the moment, fixed income returns have complicated tax compliance needs. They are treated differently than equity market gains. However, there has been a lot of noise in the financial community to level the playing field between equity and fixed income gains. If this happens, it will make bonds even more attractive.

Investing in government bonds and highly rated corporate bonds for capital preservation and predictable returns in 2026 is a wise choice.
